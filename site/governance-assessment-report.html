Gap Identified
No Written Conflict of Interest Policy

The organization does not currently have a written conflict of interest policy that board members and key staff sign annually. This is a significant gap — both for IRS compliance (especially relevant for 501(c)(3) organizations) and for organizational integrity. Without a signed COI policy, the organization is exposed to legal and reputational risk. [Add specific context from their answers.]

Area for Improvement
Inconsistent Adherence to Internal Procedures

Responses indicate that internal rules and procedures are followed inconsistently — particularly during high-pressure periods. While the policies exist, enforcement and cultural normalization of these policies requires attention. [Add specific context.]

Recommendations
1
Immediate Priority
Draft and Adopt a Conflict of Interest Policy

Work with legal counsel to draft a conflict of interest policy that meets IRS standards for 501(c)(3) organizations. Require annual signature from all board members and key staff. Civic Sphere can provide a template policy as a starting point. Target: within 60 days.

2
Near-Term (90 Days)
Establish Bylaw Review Cycle

Schedule a formal bylaw review as a standing agenda item every 3 years, with a board vote to approve. Document the review date in meeting minutes. This ensures governance documents remain current as the organization evolves.
